## Abstract

Autoregressive graph generation is commonly formulated by linearizing a graph into a sequence via a flat walk over its nodes and edges. While effective for small molecular graphs, such representations do not scale to large, geometrically complex biological graphs, where higher-order structure is both dense and functionally significant. This limitation reflects the pitfalls of flat linearizations as a tokenization scheme, as they fail to expose hierarchical organization and thus restrict the model to an overly local representation. To address this, we study sequence constructions that encode hierarchy in the tokenization, comparing a flat representation with two alternatives: one that explicitly factorizes communities and their interactions, and another that induces hierarchy through a depth-first traversal. Through an extensive empirical study, we find that performance is comparable on simple graphs but diverges significantly on complex domains (e.g., cyclic peptides and proteins). Flat representations degrade in validity or distributional fidelity, while the traversal-based hierarchical representation remains stable and on-distribution; in contrast, the explicitly factorized variant underperforms, suggesting that hierarchy is most effective when embedded in the generation process rather than imposed as a separate structure. Finally, the coarse-to-fine structure of the traversal-based approach enables motif-conditioned generation without architectural changes: conditioning on higher-level prefixes yields strong motif retention along with high validity, uniqueness, and novelty, whereas flat representations exhibit severe mode collapse under comparable constraints.
